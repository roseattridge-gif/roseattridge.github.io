02 · How I help

The signature engagement

When the problem is too complex to hold in separate meetings, reports and people’s heads—put it on the wall.

This is not a consultancy framework invented for a website. It is the way I have solved difficult operational problems throughout my career: get close to the work, listen properly, crack out the brown paper and Post-it notes, and make the problem visible enough for people to solve it together.

Why the wall works

You can argue with a report. It is much harder to ignore a system you have built together.

I have spent my career pulling operations apart and putting them back together—through Lean Six Sigma Black Belt work, business improvement, customer experience, data, governance and transformation. Different sectors, different problems, same recurring lesson.

The breakthrough nearly always comes when the brown paper goes up and the Post-it notes come out. Suddenly, the process is no longer trapped in somebody’s head, buried in a slide deck or split across a dozen meetings. People can point to it. Challenge it. Add the missing piece. See where the work doubles back, where a handover fails and where the organisation is quietly relying on one person to hold everything together.

Most importantly, it stops being my diagnosis or leadership’s diagnosis. The people in the room build a shared understanding they recognise as their own. That is why the priorities stick—and why the conversation moves from defending individual perspectives to fixing the system.

04 · The approach

How the work moves

Go to the work. Bring the pieces together. Decide what happens next.

  1. 01

    Go to the frontline

    Spend time where the work actually happens and understand how the challenge looks from inside the system.

  2. 02

    Listen without polishing

    Interview people across roles and levels, preserving the friction, contradictions and uncomfortable evidence.

  3. 03

    Put it on the wall

    Externalise the system physically so everyone can see, challenge and add to one shared working picture.

  4. 04

    Prioritise together

    Work through what must change first, why it matters, who owns it and what a credible sequence of action looks like.
