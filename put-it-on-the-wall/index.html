Why the wall

The wall was never a metaphor.

Rose has spent most of her career as a Lean Six Sigma Black Belt, taking complex operations apart and helping organisations put them back together again.

Across operational improvement, the development of an AI governance and decision framework for a large FTSE 250 organisation, and more recent work in complex advanced engineering, the turning point has repeatedly been the same:

Get the right people around a wall. Make the work visible. Build one shared picture.

Brown paper and Post-it notes are not workshop theatre. They create a place where evidence, frontline experience, assumptions, contradictions and difficult questions can exist together.

The people doing the work can see where they fit. Their knowledge is counted. Different interpretations can be challenged openly. And the eventual decision belongs to the people who must make it work — not only to the person who produced the presentation.

Put It On The Wall turns that repeatedly proven way of working into a structured decision system organisations can operate for themselves.

Questions worth asking

No theatre. No mystery.

Does every programme end with an AI recommendation?

No. A credible No-AI decision is a valuable outcome. The purpose is to choose the intervention that creates value, not to justify technology already selected.

Is this an AI strategy workshop?

No. It is a six-week AI opportunity assessment and decision system. Live sessions are used only where people need to resolve different interpretations, make trade-offs or commit ownership.

How is this different from AI use-case prioritisation?

Conventional use-case prioritisation often starts with a list of ideas. AI Opportunity Wall starts with evidence, validates the underlying business problem and compares AI with credible non-AI alternatives before anything is prioritised.

Is this consultancy or a toolkit?

Neither in the usual sense. It is a fully designed team journey: Rose frames the moments that require expert judgement, while an internal facilitator operates the runbook inside the organisation.

What does the programme cost?

Founding-team programmes start from £15,000 + VAT. If the programme is appropriate, Rose sends a fixed-fee scope covering the six-week journey, dates, internal roles, support boundaries and definition of done.

Who needs to be involved?

You need an accountable sponsor carrying the decision risk, one capable internal facilitator and a small cross-functional group with access to the relevant evidence and operating reality.

What happens in the first conversation?

We test whether the problem is significant, cross-functional and genuinely undecided; whether there is a sponsor carrying the risk; and whether AI Opportunity Wall is the right next move. You will get a candid answer.
